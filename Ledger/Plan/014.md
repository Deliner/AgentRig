# P014 — Explainable and verifiable consumer workflows

## Feature

Make the effective installed AgentRig workflow explainable and verify it through consumer installation, normal operation, update and interruption recovery. Deliver the three user-selected additions using existing command, routing, installation and testing owners.

## User capability

The user supplies a path or supported event and understands which hooks, skills, rules and checks apply and why. Independent consumer tests demonstrate that the installed workflow behaves that way after setup and update, and interrupted operations can be resumed without losing unrelated work.

## Acceptance

- Explanation (selected idea 1): expose a documented command through existing CLI/catalog/Just interfaces. Explain applicable hook handlers/routes and their sources, skills, lint rules, relevant checks/test selection and exclusion or non-selection reasons from effective configuration. Reuse existing lint-explain and routing/selection owners rather than a second policy evaluator.
- Explanation does not execute target hooks, commands, tests or edits, consume reminders, advance retries or change policy. Distinguish determined outcomes from conditional behavior requiring runtime context; do not claim to predict arbitrary external hook programs. Do not expose secrets. Verify representative overlapping routes, exclusions and missing-input behavior against actual dispatch and selection.
- Portability (selected idea 3): extend existing consumer tests with the full scenario: create a project outside the source checkout, install the distribution, exercise registered hooks and lint through installed entry points, update the environment, and repeat relevant checks. Verify supported harness/VCS differences without an exhaustive Cartesian product of equivalent cases.
- Consumers use their own configuration, layouts, skills, memory and runtime paths and do not depend on this repository's policy, source-tree imports or cached development installation. Preserve user-owned settings/resources. Document prerequisites; hook registration and lint tests do not require paid model calls.
- Recovery (selected idea 4): extend interruption coverage at relevant setup, upgrade and check boundaries. Reach an observable boundary, interrupt the owned process, inspect recovery state and retry through supported public operations. Verify preservation of unrelated edits, truthful incomplete/failed verification evidence and clear recovery guidance. Successful retry leaves the intended usable environment and effective hooks.
- Synchronize interruption with observable operation state rather than arbitrary delays. Reuse journals, test executables and consumer fixtures, adding only a minimal seam where necessary for a credible interruption case. Do not invent a general failure-injection framework or promise recovery from arbitrary disk corruption.
- Reconcile existing coverage first. Independent delivery, repeated setup, mixed-installation recovery and interrupted upgrade verification already have tests. Extend or consolidate them instead of duplicating suites. Resolve failures directly within these accepted scenarios as part of delivery.
- Keep workflow tests with their owners and provide a documented focused invocation. Maintain affected commit groups and full integration verification. P013 lint evaluations retain their scope. No independent tracing service, event-history registry or new test framework is required. Policy before/after simulation (idea 2) and mutation testing of evals (idea 5) are not included.
