# D131 — Error on a derived Deserialize beside a checking constructor

## Context

B080 (user, 2026-11-07, after measuring a list of smells on the 7.8 million line
corpus of `agentrig-research/scratchpad/meta/experiments/list-rules/a1-ctor/report.md`):
7 of 9 sites are useful, six of them panics, such as `Interval` in bevy and
`IntRange` in nushell. By `type-driven-discipline` a value with a validity
condition is built through one checking entry point, and `derive(Deserialize)`
opens a second entry point that skips the check.

## Chosen

A new rule kind `unchecked-deserialize` in the `Signature` family (D133) with the
repair skill `check-deserialize`, shipped at `error`. A struct item deriving
`Deserialize` in an attribute above it, directly or inside `cfg_attr`, is
measured with the `impl` blocks of the same file. A checking constructor is a
function without `self` in the type's inherent `impl`, `impl TryFrom` or
`impl FromStr` that returns a `Result` or `Option` of `Self` or the type name,
tests a parameter by an `if` whose branch returns `Err` or `None`, calls `bail!`
or `panic!` or ends in `Err` or `None`, by `ensure!`, or by `then` or
`then_some` on an expression naming it, and stores that parameter, directly or
through `into`, `to_owned`, `to_string`, `clone` or `into_owned`, in a named or
positional field without serde `deserialize_with` or `with`. One finding per
struct on its declaration line with the symbol `Type::constructor`. No finding
with serde `try_from` or `from` on the struct, a manual `impl Deserialize` for
it in the file, or in test code. The detector sits beside `secret-debug` in
`languages/rust/derived_trait.rs` and shares its attribute walk; `type_name` and
`plain_name` move to `languages/rust/context` for it and `ord-eq-mismatch`.
Two-phase delivery with a mid-branch pin (D051).

## Rejected

A `DECISION:` or `INVARIANT:` justification above the struct: the card lists no
such exemption, and `serde(try_from = …)` repairs every case. Any fallible
constructor without a tested and stored parameter: a failure from I/O or parsing
of another value is not a validity condition of a field. Constructors in other
files: the card leaves them unseen, and following them needs module resolution.
Enums: the card covers named and tuple structs.

## Rationale

The derive, the constructor, its refusal and the stored field are visible in one
file's syntax, so the rule needs no type resolution (D059). Requiring the tested
parameter to reach a field keeps the finding on a value the check guards.

## Consequences

A consumer's non-test Rust struct deriving `Deserialize` beside a checking
constructor fails lint on `init` and before commit. A constructor in another
file, a check through a helper function, and enums stay unseen. It ships B080
in full; it supersedes nothing.
