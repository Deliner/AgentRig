# D155 — Structural bans of B105: the forms that pass the precision bar and the owner of each form

## Context

B105 under D151 lists twelve Rust forms from the bans lists of 2026-11-10 that Clippy does not check and syntax can
decide: II-13 a `..base` struct update, II-20 a `String` error type, II-29 `static mut`, II-30 `Deserialize` without
`deny_unknown_fields`, III-10 `#[cfg]` on a statement, III-11 a derived order without explicit discriminants, III-15 an
unlabeled exit of a nested loop, III-16 a subtraction from `len()`, III-18 a sentinel integer result, III-19 a default
in place of a parse error, III-23 `Mutex<()>` and III-28 `spawn` as a statement. AC1 ships a rule only when its
precision is at least 90% on the rig and on voxel-rust and records the rest. The card says that each form gets its own
rule kind and repair skill and that each form has one owner.

A protocol fixed before any finding was seen classified every finding of every detector in frozen copies of both trees,
test code excluded: true when the card's hazard applies at the site and its «Оставить» category does not. A form with
no finding in either tree is not measurable and ships under D151 with behavioral tests.

## Chosen

Four new rule kinds at error level, outside test code, each kept by a `DECISION:` or `INVARIANT:` reference on the line
directly above the site, or above an item's attributes:

- `unlabeled-exit` (III-15): a `break` or `continue` without a label whose nearest loop lies inside another loop of the
  same function; closures, nested functions and async blocks bound both searches. Repair skill `label-the-exit`.
- `length-subtraction` (III-16): a binary `-` whose left operand is a `.len()` or `.count()` call without arguments.
  Repair skill `subtract-checked`.
- `implicit-order` (III-11): an enum of two or more variants deriving `PartialOrd` or `Ord`, directly or inside
  `cfg_attr`, with a variant lacking an explicit discriminant. Repair skill `pin-the-order`.
- `unit-lock` (III-23): a `Mutex<()>` or `RwLock<()>` type from any path, and a `Mutex::new(())` or `RwLock::new(())`
  that initializes, directly or as a call argument, a `let` binding without a type, so one site is reported once.
  Repair skill `lock-the-data`.

The control-flow detectors form `languages/rust/control_flow` beside the signature and ownership families; the rules
directory's dependents budget rises from 11 to 12 for that new consumer of the rule identities. `implicit-order` and
`derived_trait` share `derives`, and the item exemptions share `justified_item`, in the Rust context module.

Two forms keep an existing owner instead of a kind of their own, by the card's boundary of one owner per form:

- III-28 extends `discarded-result` (D090, D099, D114) and its skill `handle-discarded`: a bare statement or a `let _ =`
  binding whose value, through `?`, `.unwrap()` and `.expect(..)`, is a call whose path or method ends in `spawn`.
- II-29 stays with `unsafe-justification`, which flags every `static mut` without a reference since D103.

## Rejected

Shipping the other six forms. Their false findings fall in categories that syntax cannot tell from the hazard:

- II-13 `struct-update`: option sets whose contract is "the rest by default", such as wgpu descriptors and protobuf
  messages.
- II-20 `string-error`: helpers of a code generator or a profiling binary whose error is only printed.
- II-30 `unknown-fields`: partial views of an external format such as `Cargo.toml`, whose unknown keys must pass. Its
  overlap with `unchecked-deserialize` therefore needs no resolution.
- III-10 `statement-cfg`: statements naming modules or fields that exist only under the same `cfg`.
- III-18 `sentinel-value`: FFI return codes, where the card keeps the sentinel as part of the format.
- III-19, an extension of `silent-error` to a default after `parse`, `try_from`, `try_into` or `from_str`: a
  deliberate clamp of a numeric conversion.

Narrowing a detector against the measured sites to lift its precision: the same sites would then both tune and judge
it.

A separate kind and skill for III-28 and II-29: a second rule would report the same site as its owner.

## Rationale

A rule that is wrong at more than one site in ten teaches agents to justify findings instead of repairing them. A
fixed protocol keeps the verdict independent of the detector's author, and one owner per form keeps one finding and one
repair per site.

## Consequences

The four kinds are delivered inert first and then enabled in the repository and through `init` with the rig's findings
repaired (D051). The `spawn` form applies wherever `discarded-result` is configured as soon as a runtime carrying it is
pinned. The six rejected forms remain review norms in the bans lists; a later decision may add one with a different
detector measured anew. The measured counts are recorded in the delivery of P113.
