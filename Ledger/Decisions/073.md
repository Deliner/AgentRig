# D073 — B031 meaningful-identifier and magic-number checks stay norms

## Context

B031 (cluster 6 of the quality set, agreed 2026-10-29), besides the dump-name rule
shipped as D072, proposes two further candidates: 6.2 — identifiers carry meaning
(ban `tmp`/`data`/single-letter names outside small scopes); 6.3 — numeric
literals other than 0/1 go through a named constant. The card marks 6.2 as
false-positive-prone ("может остаться мягким/нормой") and its boundary routes a
noisy candidate to a norm with a recorded reason (norm-or-choice). The structural
engine is purely syntactic (D035) and omits the scope and type resolution a
type-resolving frontend would add (D059).

## Chosen

6.2 and 6.3 stay norms, not init rules. Neither meets the
near-zero-false-positive, high-signal bar a gate rule requires (D035). Only the
decidable dump-name check (6.1) ships as a rule (D072).

## Rejected

A 6.2 rule: separating a legitimate short-lived or loop-local name from a
meaningless module-level one needs scope analysis the syntactic engine does not do,
and `data`, `tmp` and single letters are common legitimate names (`i`/`j` loop
indices, a `data` payload), so a blanket ban is high-false-positive. A 6.3 rule:
numeric literals that are genuinely magic cannot be separated from legitimate ones
(indices, sizes, bit widths, status codes, time units, powers of two) without
context, which is why clippy and ruff keep magic-value lints in opt-in noisy
groups; a blanket rule would train authors to introduce pointless single-use
constants, and the comment ban (D037) does not change that calculus because the
named-constant fix is itself often the noise. A weakened or heavily-excepted
version would relax the high-signal bar (D035) the whole lint set depends on.

## Rationale

Norm-or-choice: a rule fits a decidable, high-signal check; a norm fits a behavior
that cannot be checked statically without false positives or a disproportionate
subsystem. 6.1 is decidable (D072); 6.2 and 6.3 are not, so they stay norms with
this recorded reason rather than noisy rules.

## Consequences

B031 ships one init rule, the dump-name check (6.1, D072). Meaningful identifiers
(6.2) and no-magic-numbers (6.3) remain author guidance, not enforced by the gate;
no new rule kind, skill or invariant is added for them. Reassessing 6.2 or 6.3 as a
rule would require the scope/type-resolution frontend deferred by D059. It
supersedes nothing.
