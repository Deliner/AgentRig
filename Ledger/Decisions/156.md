# D156 — Structural bans of B106: the forms that pass the precision bar, mixed junctions and configured call pairs

## Context

B106 under D151 lists twenty-two Rust forms from the bans lists of 2026-11-10 that Clippy does not check and syntax
can decide. AC1 ships a rule only when its precision is at least 90% on the rig and on voxel-rust and records the rest;
AC2 repairs the rig's findings; AC3 has `init` install the rules. The card gives each form its own rule kind and repair
skill, keeps one owner per form and makes II-7, a mix of `&&` and `||`, a parameter of the existing
`boolean-expression` rule instead of a rule of its own.

The method of D155 was reused: a protocol fixed before any finding was seen classified every finding of every detector
in frozen copies of both trees, test code excluded, true when the card's hazard applies at the site and its «Оставить»
category does not. A form with no finding in either tree is not measurable and ships under D151 with behavioral tests.
Two amendments preceded any classification: voxel's `src/target/**`, Cargo build output that voxel's own `.gitignore`
ignores, lies outside the measured code; and the visible-field form reports one finding per struct, named by its first
checking constructor, instead of one per field.

## Chosen

Fourteen new rule kinds at error level, outside test code, each kept by a `DECISION:` or `INVARIANT:` reference on the
line directly above the site, or above an item's attributes.

Signature forms, detected in `languages/rust/signature`:

- `same-type-parameters` (II-2): a fn item or signature with two non-receiver parameters of token-equal type, skipping
  fns inside `impl Trait for`; one finding per fn. Skill `name-the-arguments`.
- `same-type-positions` (II-3): a tuple struct or tuple variant with two fields of token-equal type; a returned tuple
  stays with `tuple-as-record`. Skill `name-the-positions`.
- `primitive-alias` (II-5): a type alias without generics of a numeric primitive, `char` or `String`. Skill
  `wrap-the-primitive`.
- `ambiguous-option` (II-22): an `Option<bool>` or `Option<Option<..>>`. Skill `name-the-states`.
- `partial-impl` (II-14): a manual `PartialEq`, `Hash`, `PartialOrd`, `Ord` or `Clone` that reads a `self` field without
  a pattern or literal of the type naming every field. Skill `destructure-the-fields`.
- `constant-default` (III-8): a trait method whose nonempty default body does not mention `self`. Skill
  `require-the-method`.

Ownership forms, detected in `languages/rust/ownership` except the first, which shares D131's checking-constructor
definition in the derived-trait module:

- `open-checked-field` (III-1): a struct whose checking constructor stores a checked parameter in a field with a
  visibility modifier; one finding per struct. Skill `hide-checked-field`.
- `wrapper-deref` (III-2): `Deref` or `DerefMut` for a struct of the same file whose `Target` is not its type parameter.
  Skill `delegate-not-deref`.
- `primitive-from` (III-3): `From` of a numeric primitive, `bool`, `char`, `String` or `&str` for a one-field struct of
  the same file. Skill `construct-explicitly`.
- `paired-calls` (III-26): a statement calling the open method of a configured pair, then in the same block an early
  exit (`?`, `return`, `break` or `continue` outside closures), then the close method on the token-equal receiver. The
  rule's `pairs` setting lists the pairs and is required, nonempty, with distinct open and close names; at most one
  rule of a configuration lists pairs. The example writes begin/commit, lock/unlock, open/close, start/stop,
  enter/leave and acquire/release. Skill `guard-the-pair`.

Control-flow forms, detected in `languages/rust/control_flow`:

- `variant-equality` (III-6): `==` or `!=` with a path of two or more segments ending in an UpperCamelCase name, and a
  `matches!` whose pattern names such a path. Skill `match-every-variant`.
- `range-chain` (III-7): an `if`/`else if` chain of two or more conditions, or a `match` with two or more guards,
  comparing one token-equal expression with an integer literal by `<`, `<=`, `>` or `>=`. Skill `match-the-ranges`.

Reference forms, detected in `languages/rust/naming.rs`, one file for the two forms that tie a value to its slot by
position instead of a name:

- `positional-format` (III-12): a format-family macro whose first string literal has two or more unnamed placeholders.
  Skill `name-the-placeholders`.
- `crossed-field` (III-13): a struct literal field initialized by a plain identifier that names another field of the
  same literal. Skill `align-field-names`.

II-7 is the `junctions` setting of `boolean-expression`. `mixed`, the default, keeps D120's operand count alone;
`single` also reports at error level every outermost Rust `&&`/`||` or Python `and`/`or` expression that joins both
junctions, directly or inside parentheses, because under D120 parentheses group operands without ending the
expression. The repository and `init` use `single`; the repair is in `split-boolean-expression`.

A site that must keep its form keeps a reference to this decision: a signature fixed by a foreign side such as JNI or
an ABI, or a comparison with a variant of a foreign enum closed by definition such as `std::path::Component`. A
comparison with a variant of an open foreign enum such as `io::ErrorKind` is a true finding; a full match over every
stable variant does not fit the 40-line function limit at each site, so the comparison needs one owner that carries
the reference, or a reference at each site.

## Rejected

Shipping seven forms whose false findings fall in categories syntax cannot tell from the hazard:

- III-14 `nested-shadowing`: unwrapping a value into the same name through a combinator closure, `x.map(|x| ..)`.
- III-30 `loop-flag`: parser state flags and loops that do work on every step.
- III-29 `manual-counter`: a nesting depth that also decrements, and a position counting a filtered subset.
- III-24 `string-key`: file-path keys of a files map and keys of an external TOML format.
- III-21 `state-with-options`: a diagnostic level beside optional values unrelated to it.
- III-25 `optional-collection`: `None` meaning not found or failed, distinct from an empty collection.
- III-4 `mutable-getter`: a mutable view of state independent of the other fields.

A separate rule kind for II-7: the card makes it a parameter, and a second rule would report the expression that
`boolean-expression` already owns.

Counting `io::ErrorKind` comparisons false because the enum is `#[non_exhaustive]`: the protocol's false category is an
enum closed by definition, and Clippy's `wildcard_enum_match_arm`, denied by D153, lists every stable variant of
`ErrorKind`, so a full match names a variant std adds later and the card's hazard applies.

A fixed built-in list of call pairs: names such as `open` and `start` mean different operations in different projects,
so each consumer states its pairs.

Narrowing a detector against the measured sites to lift its precision, as in D155.

## Rationale

As in D155, a rule wrong at more than one site in ten teaches agents to justify findings instead of repairing them, a
fixed protocol keeps the verdict independent of the detector's author, and one owner per form keeps one finding and
one repair per site. Configured pairs keep the detector free of one project's vocabulary.

## Consequences

The fourteen kinds and the `single` setting are delivered inert first and then enabled in the repository and through
`init` with the rig's findings repaired (D051). The measured counts are recorded in the delivery of P114. The seven
rejected forms remain review norms in the bans lists; a later decision may add one with a different detector measured
anew.
